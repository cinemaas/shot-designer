To scale, not to taste

Twenty units to the foot. A fifteen-foot car is fifteen feet, an 18-inch shoulder is 18 inches, and a lens cone is the real angle for that focal length on your format.

Blocking that moves

Click out where somebody walks. Each move has its own start and length, so one person can cross while another waits and a camera can begin its move halfway through.

Rigs with their mechanics

Dolly, dolly and jib, slider — and track in the lengths and angles it actually comes in, so the plan tells you what to order.

Shot lists, fast

Type the shorthand you already write. Lay out standard coverage in a click, and send the list to a Google Sheet with pictures.

Look down the lens before you carry anything

Every camera has a real lens on a real format at a real height. Open the viewfinder and you get a rough, honest picture of the room from there — enough to settle whether she's behind the sofa without moving a light.

Fly it from the panel: height, tilt and pan on sliders, with the overhead turning as you drag.

  • Storyboard — drop the frame on the plan
  • Save PNG — or a still of every beat, in a folder
  • Brief — the shot written out, measured off the scene

It reads the files you already have

Scenes are .hcw, read and written in place. A library you already own opens as it is, and anything you save still opens anywhere else that reads the format — byte for byte.

It saves itself while you work, and keeps every previous version.
